Counting rule for the category fractions: each participant × watched video × individual cue interval is one opportunity. One or several selected moments inside the same cue’s ±5-second window count as one detected cue. Separate cue intervals remain separate opportunities, including overlapping intervals.
Do not interpret the fraction as a detection percentage: the numerator is the number of selected timestamps, while the denominator is the average number of real cue intervals in the case’s two salience versions. Direct-hit opportunity view

How many available cues were marked at least once?

Each participant × watched video × individual cue interval contributes one opportunity. Multiple relevant-moment selections by the same participant inside the same cue interval still count as one hit. Cases are ranked from highest to lowest using each case's pooled direct-hit rate across all displayed cue opportunities.

What “Pooled total” means: For each case, the detected opportunities from cause, debugging process, error type, and motivational-affective trajectory are added together. Their available opportunities are also added together. The pooled percentage is then sum(detected opportunities) ÷ sum(available opportunities) × 100. For example, if the four cue types together contain 18 detected opportunities out of 60 available opportunities, the pooled total is 30%. This is the value used to rank cases. It is not the simple average of the four cue-type percentages, because cue types with more available intervals appropriately contribute more to the total.

Percentage of available opportunities hit

Detected direct-hit opportunities divided by all participant–video–cue opportunities for that case and cue type. The final Pooled total column is the value used for ranking: all detected opportunities across cue types divided by all available opportunities across cue types. It is weighted by opportunity count and is not a simple average of the four cue-type percentages.

Raw detected / available opportunities

Labels expose the denominator. For example, 6 / (4 × 3) means 6 detected opportunities from 4 cue intervals shown to 3 participants. The pooled column shows the summed numerator and denominator across all cue types.

Combined-salience denominator: When s0 and s1 contain different numbers of cue intervals or were watched by different numbers of participants, the denominator is a sum—for example, 6 / ((4 × 3) + (3 × 2)). It is not the number of marked timestamps: repeated marks within the same participant–video–cue interval count once.

Duration adjusted — ±5 seconds

What the percentage means: detected participant–video–cue intervals ÷ available participant–video–cue intervals × 100. For example, 36.1% for cause means that participants marked a moment within the inclusive ±5-second matching window of 36.1% of the cause intervals they were shown. It is not the percentage of participants, all selected moments, or video time. Intervals labelled cue_type = none are excluded completely and are treated as uncued time.

Worked example: cause = 36.111%

Of all cause-cue intervals shown to participants in their assigned videos, 36.111% had at least one participant-selected moment in the cue interval or its ±5-second reaction window.

Important details

  • If three participants watched a video containing one cause cue, that produces three opportunities.
  • Two marks by the same participant in the same cue matching window count only once.
  • Two separate cause intervals in one video are separate opportunities.
  • It is not the percentage of all marked moments.
  • It is not the percentage of participants who recognized cause cues.
  • It is not the percentage of video time.

About none: rows explicitly labelled cue_type = "none" describe uncued spans, not cues. This version excludes them completely from cue counts, durations, timelines, opportunities, and cue-detection graphs. A selected moment that matches no real cue even after applying the ±5-second tolerance is treated as no_cue.

Motivation is consistently overlooked, especially in written diagnoses, but also when participants mark relevant moments. Although the sample is small, this pattern is consistent with findings reported in other studies [citation needed].

Final diagnoses adjusted for cue availability

The value is the number of coded diagnosis mentions divided by the matching cue instances available in the videos represented in valid diagnosis rows. Unlike the relevant-moment detection percentage, this is a mention density and is not a unique-cue detection percentage.

Open full-resolution plot ↗

Three different denominators—do not read them as the same percentage

  • Relevant-moment cue detection: unique participant–video–cue intervals marked at least once ÷ available participant–video–cue intervals. This is a percentage capped at 100%.
  • Diagnosis mention density in the plot above: coded diagnosis-theme mentions ÷ matching available cue intervals. For example, 0.636 means 0.636 mentions per available interval—not 63.6% recognized—and the value can exceed 1.
  • Recognition completeness on the new subpage: complete or partial intended dimensions ÷ all intended dimensions coded across valid participant–video diagnoses. Absent and wrong are not counted as found. This is also a percentage capped at 100%.

The recognized_intended_cues column changes only the third measure. It does not change the relevant-moment plot or the diagnosis mention-density plot. Rows labelled cue_type = "none" are excluded from all cue-based denominators and plots.

How to interpret the cue-detection percentage

Example: cause = 36.111%.

Of all cause-cue intervals shown to participants in their assigned videos, 36.111% had at least one selected moment in the cue interval or its ±5-second reaction window.
  • Matching uses the inclusive window from cue start − 5 seconds through cue end + 5 seconds.
  • Three participants watching a video with one cause cue produce three opportunities.
  • Two marks by the same participant in the same matching window count once.
  • Two separate cause intervals in one video are separate opportunities.
  • The value is not the percentage of marked moments, participants, or video time.

The tolerance affects matching only; cue counts, durations, timelines, and overlap checks use the original boundaries. Rows labelled cue_type = "none" are uncued spans and are excluded completely. A selection matching no real cue after tolerance is classified as no_cue.

Recognition of intended cues

This page uses recognized_intended_cues in the fixed order error type, cause, debugging process, and motivational-affective trajectory. Accepted codes are c/complete, p/partial, a/absent, and w/wrong.

Ccomplete: the intended cue dimension was fully articulated
Ppartial: the intended cue dimension was mentioned incompletely
Aabsent: the intended cue dimension was not articulated
Wwrong: the diagnosis articulated an incorrect interpretation of the intended dimension
How to read the heatmaps: Every cell prints the percentages coded Complete, Partial, Absent, and Wrong. The ordered background score is W = 0, A = 1/3, P = 2/3, and C = 1. Colors progress from dark red for wrong through orange for absent and yellow for partial to bright green for complete. Interpret the printed C/P/A/W percentages as the primary result.

Exploratory interpretation

Patterns worth investigating

These observations summarize the current descriptive data. They are prompts for analysis, not confirmatory statistical conclusions.

Dominant cue match

debugging process is the most frequent classification with 307 matched selections. Check whether this reflects cue availability, cue duration, or participant attention.

Selections outside defined cues

6.4% of unique selected moments fall outside all cue intervals. Those moments may reveal missing cue definitions or different participant priorities.

Salience comparison

s1 has the higher descriptive mean (s0: 17.2; s1: 18.0). Compare within case before interpreting the aggregate difference.

Coverage and selection volume

A moderate positive descriptive relationship (r = 0.44). This association is expected to be partly mechanical because more viewers create more opportunities to select moments.

Interpretation boundary: The raw plots are not normalized; the Adjusted rates view normalizes by cue count or cue duration within participant-video exposure. Repeated selections remain distinct. Use these descriptive rates to locate patterns, then test them with an analysis matched to the study design.
